Credibility before capital. The rules this package holds itself to.

A century of this field is littered with frauds and self-deception. The company rejects far more than it pursues, and it labels everything it keeps. These are the rules; a reader who finds a page breaking one of them has found a defect, and we would like to hear about it.

1 · The two-question filter
Is there a net source? Is there a colder sink? A claim that cannot answer both is not advanced, promoted or funded — it is documented and set down. This is the instrument that caught a fraudulent vendor during the company’s own diligence in 2025.
2 · Six labels for every third-party result

3 · Numbers

No watt, efficiency, runtime or performance figure is attributed to an EnergyWave device anywhere in this package, because none exists. Every number carries its origin: assumption · calculation · proposed target · company measurement · attributed published result. A superlative is a claim that needs a citation instead.

4 · The boundary the program is built to test

Legitimate adjacent science — the Casimir effect, the Lamb shift, metamaterials and nanofabrication, ion-wind and field propulsion — is kept explicitly separate from the contested leap: continuous net extraction of energy from the vacuum, which the rigorous reviews say would violate the second law of thermodynamics, and whose kilowatt figures in the literature are unbuilt theoretical estimates. The line between the two is exactly where the company does its work.

5 · Publish in both directions

If a gate kills an effect, the company says so, in public, with the error budget attached. A retired claim is a deliverable. Investors read the same report the public does.

6 · What stays out of public and government-facing material until cleared

A patent number; a device performance specification; any statement that a device exists; any person’s name without their written consent; any concept not yet defined, sourced and tested against the two-question standard.
