Safe release by design. If a high-density power source for humanoid robots ever exists, this is how it ships.

In August 2025 a paper on McLean Companies letterhead proposed a safe, government-guided commercial release of a power source for AI-driven humanoid robots. Its purpose stands. Its performance figures, its named business partner and its entity do not: this version carries the purpose forward under EnergyWave’s name and claims discipline, and the original stays in the archive, unedited, as a legacy record.

What this version withdraws, explicitly. The 2025 paper stated as fact a 25 kW continuous output, a 3.5 kg / 0.008 m³ package, an energy density “equivalent to 50 MJ/kg”, prototypes with “99.98% uptime over 3,000 hours”, and expert confirmation that the technology was ready for market. None of those statements is supported by any document the company holds. EnergyWave has no prototype, no measured output and no validated energy-conversion mechanism (DoW white paper v2.0, Statement of Claims Discipline). 1 · Executive summary

Humanoid robots are a power problem before they are an intelligence problem. To sense and move like a person a platform must run cameras and microphones, chemical sensing, tactile skin and dense actuation at once, and the industry’s current answer to endurance is logistics — swap packs, dock, run in shifts — rather than energy. EnergyWave is a research venture built to find out, to a published standard, whether any non-chemical approach can change that; and, separately, to design the control and safety architecture that any high-density portable source would need before it could be released at all. This paper is about the second track. It proposes that the release architecture be developed with government visibility from the start, under binding scope exclusions, so that if the research track ever produces something, the question of safe release has already been answered.

3 · What EnergyWave does instead of claiming

Every candidate mechanism the company examines passes three gates in order — characterize, conservation-test, extract or retire publicly — with pre-registered protocols, replication contracted before each gate, an external advisory board with veto over any performance statement, and negative results published under the company’s own name. The first statement of a number, ever, would follow Gate-3 third-party validation. The research program is described in Research Program and Gates and is not repeated here. Nothing in this paper depends on that program succeeding.

4 · An illustrative scenario — illustration, not a product

A household humanoid learns a dish from a kitchen on the other side of the world over a satellite link, checks the pantry, orders what is missing within a budget, and has a hot meal ready when the owner’s phone says they are ten minutes from home. Nothing in that scenario is exotic except the power budget: the platform runs human-grade sensing and dense actuation for hours without a dock.

Kept from the 2025 paper as the kind of continuous, simultaneous load the company means by “a humanoid is a power problem”. It asserts no capability. The quantitative version is the Humanoid Power Budget Specification in 03_Technical_Documents.

5 · Control and safety architecture — developed on its own track
PrincipleDesign
Platform bindingThe module is scoped to ambulatory robotic platforms and cryptographically bound to one host; it authenticates to the platform controller and delivers no output to an unrecognized host — a keyed consumable, like a cartridge and its printer.
Scoping in hardwarePower ceiling, ramp rate and duty envelope fixed in hardware to the actuation and compute envelope of an ambulatory platform. Repurposing as a bulk supply means defeating hardware limits — a detectable and destructive act.
Tamper-responsive inertionA tamper-response boundary of the kind standard in high-assurance cryptographic hardware. Envelope breach, unauthorized disassembly, out-of-spec thermal or electrical conditions, or authentication failure trigger an irreversible transition to an inert state — the “paperweight” test. No service mode, no recovery path.
Custody and revocationUnits individually identified, attested and remotely revocable. A recovered or diverted unit is a dead unit.
CompartmentalizationKnow-how and any future hardware held in imageable / process / state bins; split manufacturing; post-quantum platform binding — Compartmentalization & Anti-Tamper Posture v0.1 (Sept 2026), counsel questions in its §8 still open.
Negative scope — offered as binding conditions of any government relationship, not as a statement in a paper. No directed-energy application. No propulsion application. No munitions or ordnance integration. No general-purpose or grid-scale power configuration. No export or foreign transfer absent explicit U.S. Government authorization. The 2025 paper’s own exclusions — “excluding laser cannons or exotic propulsion” — are kept and widened.

7 · Why government collaboration, and why now rather than later
  • Safety validation. An energy-density improvement of the magnitude that would matter is dual-use by construction. The appropriate time to establish government visibility is before results exist, not after.
  • Proliferation control. Custody, revocation and scope restriction are easier to design in than to retrofit; the company would rather be inside a framework early than negotiate one under pressure.
  • A disciplined negative is also a deliverable. If the research track retires every candidate, the control architecture, the measurement platform and the published negatives remain, and the question is closed in public rather than recirculated for another decade.

9 · The ask

A technical review meeting on four questions: whether the ambulatory-platform power constraint in §2 is recognized as a capability gap within the relevant program offices; whether a conservation-tested characterization program with public retirement criteria is of interest as a low-cost, high-information investment; whether the control architecture in §5 should be developed under Government specification, independent of the energy research outcome; and what framework would govern disclosure, custody and scope restriction should the research ever produce a positive result. The company is not asking for validation of a claim.
